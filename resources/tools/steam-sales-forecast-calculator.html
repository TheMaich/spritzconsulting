Formulas and constants

Everything below is what the code does. Constants marked "unsourced" are this tool's own assumptions.

tier rate     = 0.20 (under 10k wishlists) | 0.17 (10k to 25k) | 0.15 (25k+)
    price factor  = 1 up to $10; falls linearly to 0.667 at $15; 0.667 above   (0.10 / 0.15)
    EA factor     = 0.67 for Early Access, 1 otherwise
    Week 1        = wishlists x tier rate x price factor x EA factor
    Low case      = wishlists x 0.025 x price factor x EA factor
    Month 1       = Week 1 x 1.33   (range x1.23 to x1.47)
    Year One, A   = Week 1 x 3.16   (published range x2 to x6)
    Year One, B   = wishlists x 0.70 x EA factor
    Year One      = range from min(A, B) to max(A, B); flagged if max / min is above 2
    Boxleiter     = reviews x 20 to reviews x 60   (rough cross-check)
    Follower check= followers x bucket low to followers x bucket high

    Quick answers

    How many Steam sales does a wishlist turn into?

    It depends on size. GameDiscoverCo's median Week 1 conversion was about 0.2 copies per wishlist across all games and 0.17 for launches above 10,000 wishlists (April 2024, 100+ games), and 0.15 for launches above 25,000 wishlists, or 0.10 for games priced above $10 (October 2025, partly paywalled). The calculator uses those values by wishlist tier and eases the price effect in between $10 and $15. Its low case is about 0.025, roughly one tenth of the median. Individual games vary by 10 to 20 times, so treat the median as a reference point, not a forecast.

    How do I estimate first-month and first-year Steam sales?

    Start from the Week 1 estimate. The calculator multiplies it by 1.33 to get Month 1, within a published range of 1.23 to 1.47. For year one it shows a range from two methods: Week 1 times 3.16 (GameDiscoverCo's median Year 1 to Week 1 revenue ratio, applied to units) and about 0.70 copies per wishlist (Chris Zukowski's 2023 to 2025 data, 160 reader-submitted games). When they differ by more than 2x, the page flags it.

    What is a good wishlist to sales conversion rate on Steam?

    There is no single good rate, because it falls as the wishlist count grows. The median Week 1 conversion was about 0.20 copies per wishlist across all games, 0.17 for launches above 10,000 wishlists and 0.15 above 25,000 (GameDiscoverCo, 2024 and 2025). Chris Zukowski measured launch-month copies per wishlist at 0.22 for 2023-25 releases against 0.45 for 2020-22. Treat these as reference points, not targets: individual games vary by 10 to 20 times.

    How accurate is a Steam sales estimator?

    It's directional only. The best and worst performers can differ by 10 to 20 times, and the games that outperform usually did so through things you can't model in advance, such as going viral with influencers, co-op word of mouth or being a major franchise. Check any estimate against your own Steamworks data before using it for a decision.

    Notes
    • Experimental tool. This calculator is based on publicly available industry data, but the underlying metrics are subject to significant variance, genre-specific differences, and market conditions that no dataset fully captures. Use it to explore possible scenarios and pressure-test assumptions. Don't use it as a basis for financial decisions without cross-referencing with your own data and professional advice.
    • Where the Week 1 conversion comes from. The calculator picks a median by wishlist tier: 0.20 copies per wishlist for under 10,000 wishlists (the all-games median, no tier-specific value exists), 0.17 for 10,000 to 25,000 (median for launches above 10,000 wishlists), and 0.15 for 25,000 and above. Sources: GameDiscoverCo, Apr 2024 (100+ games) and GameDiscoverCo, Oct 2025 (partly paywalled; the points cited here are visible before the paywall). For games priced above $10 the October 2025 data gives 0.10 against 0.15 overall. No source gives a figure for cheaper games, so the sub-$10 value is unsourced, and the ease between $10 and $15 is this tool's own choice that avoids a jump at $10. Gamalytic found price effects confounded with wishlist count. The low case uses about 0.025, roughly one tenth of the median, from the 2024 poll range (about one tenth to ten times the median). Results between the worst and best performers still vary by 10 to 20x.
    • What actually drives outperformance. The games that converted best in 2024 to 2025 shared two patterns: they went viral with influencers and friend groups (co-op titles especially), or they were major franchises where casual players showed up without wishlisting first. Single-player games that blew up on social video also outperformed. None of these are things you can model in advance. Source: GameDiscoverCo, Oct 2025 (partly paywalled; the points cited here are visible before the paywall)
    • How Month 1 is estimated. Gamalytic's 29 April 2024 analysis covers about 700 games released after September 2023, each with at least 5,000 wishlists and at least 500 first-month copies (units, not revenue). It finds median Month 1 sales at 27% of launch wishlists and Week 1 at 22%, which implies 1.23. That filter removes weak sellers. Pairing Chris Zukowski's Month 1 figure (0.22) with GameDiscoverCo's Week 1 median (0.15) implies 1.47. The calculator uses 1.33 as the base inside that range and shows both ends. GameDiscoverCo's 1.39x Month 1 to Week 1 ratio is a gross revenue ratio, not units. The same 75% Week 1 share assumption appears in the Wishlist Target Calculator. Sources: Gamalytic, Apr 2024, GameDiscoverCo, Apr 2024 and Chris Zukowski, How to Market a Game.
    • How Year One is estimated. Two methods, shown as a range. Method A multiplies Week 1 by 3.16, GameDiscoverCo's median Year 1 to Week 1 gross revenue ratio (range under 2x to over 6x, 100+ games, April 2024). That is a revenue ratio applied to units. Method B takes about 0.70 copies per wishlist by the end of year one for 2023-25 releases (Chris Zukowski, October 2026; 160 reader-submitted games, 34 from 2020-22 and 126 from 2023-25). The datasets differ, so the page flags the result when the methods differ by more than 2x. Viral or multiplayer titles can exceed both. Sources: GameDiscoverCo, Apr 2024, Chris Zukowski, How to Market a Game and Game World Observer, 15 Oct 2026.
    • Early Access adjustment. Gamalytic's April 2024 data (about 700 games) shows Early Access Month 1 conversion at about 20% of wishlists against about 30% for full releases, roughly one third lower. The calculator applies a factor of 0.67 to the low case, Week 1, Month 1 and Year One. Source: Gamalytic, Apr 2024
    • Inflation changed what a wishlist is worth. In Chris Zukowski's October 2026 data on 160 reader-submitted games (34 from 2020 to 2022, 126 from 2023 to 2025), copies per wishlist in the launch month fell from 0.45 to 0.22, and by the end of year one from 0.99 to about 0.70. Festival and tooltip wishlisting drive part of that. The sources disagree: GameDiscoverCo (Oct 2025, partly paywalled) reports no evidence that conversion is degrading over time, while Zukowski reports it halved. They use different samples and definitions, and this page does not resolve the conflict. Benchmarks built on older data may overstate sales. Sources: Game World Observer, 15 Oct 2026 and Chris Zukowski, How to Market a Game
    • Boxleiter cross-check. Steam Page Analyzer describes the Boxleiter method as reviews times a multiplier in a 20x to 60x range, with genre defaults of 30x to 55x, 35x for indie, and a stated accuracy of 30 to 50%. It is least reliable for very new games and works best with 50+ reviews. The multipliers describe review counts over a game's life, so a Week 1 review count is a rough cross-check only. The calculator shows the full 20x to 60x band and says whether your Week 1 figure falls inside it. Source: Steam Page Analyzer, 2026
    • Estimation only. Both sections give directional indicators for exploring scenarios. The actual outcome depends on your game quality, review scores (higher review scores convert better in Gamalytic's data), price point, genre, timing, and marketing execution. This tool has not been validated against real launches yet. Cross-check the results with your own data before any financial decision.
