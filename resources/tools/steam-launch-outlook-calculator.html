Where the game-type multipliers come from

Two coarse buckets built from six tag averages in GameDiscoverCo's 2023 survey of 125+ games (small samples): deep / systemic games (4X 7.5x, turn-based strategy 9.0x, survival 9.5x) sit around 7.5x to 9.5x, casual / narrative games (story-rich 13.2x, relaxing 15.7x, puzzle 15.9x) around 13x to 16x. If unsure, the all-game median of 12x (7x to 20x for the vast majority) is used. The 16.1x median (games released in the last 12 months with more than 50,000 launch wishlists, May 2026) is only used when the wishlist target is above 50,000 and the bucket is "Not sure".

Full numbers and caveats

Launch wishlists to end-of-Week-1 sales varies 10 to 20x between games (GameDiscoverCo, Oct 2025, partly paywalled). Recent releases may convert fewer wishlists than 2020-22 ones: Zukowski measured launch-month copies per wishlist at 0.45 (34 games, 2020-22) against 0.22 (126 games, 2023-25), while GameDiscoverCo reports no evidence of decline. Read the figures here as scenarios, not a forecast.
Model assumptions you can change (unsourced)

No published source gives follower decay constants or the spread of follower pace. These eight values are this tool's own assumptions, not data. They set the shape of the baseline and the width of the low and high cases. Change them to see how much the answer depends on them.

Formulas and constants

Everything below is what the code does. The constants marked "unsourced" are this tool's own assumptions.

pace0        = last-30-day followers / 30        (if entered; bursts inside that window are removed)
             = (followers - past bursts) / days since page open   (otherwise)
tau          = half-life / ln 2
pace(t)      = pace0 x (floor + (1 - floor) x e^(-t / tau))
organic(T)   = pace0 x (floor x T + (1 - floor) x tau x (1 - e^(-T / tau)))
burst(T)     = sum of capped boost x min(1, (T - burst day) / 5)         (5-day linear ramp)
followers(T) = followers today + pace scale x organic(T) + burst scale x burst(T)
wishlists    = followers(T) x multiplier
low  = pace x low %, bursts x low %, low multiplier
ratio        = actual wishlists / followers today (if entered; floor and ceiling = ratio -/+ spread %)
base = pace x 100%, bursts x 100%, base multiplier
high = pace x high %, bursts x 100%, high multiplier
gap          = base wishlists - target
followers needed = target / multiplier;  extra per week = (needed - projected base) / (days to launch / 7)

    Low and high stack every pessimistic or optimistic assumption together. They are a spread of scenarios, not a probability interval.

    Quick answers

    How many wishlists will my game have at launch?

    The calculator multiplies your followers by a ratio. GameDiscoverCo's surveys put the median at 12 wishlists per follower in 2023 (125+ unreleased games, range 7 to 20), and 16.1 for games above 50,000 wishlists in May 2026. It shows a floor, middle and ceiling case: deep / systemic games sit around 7.5x to 9.5x, casual / narrative games around 13x to 16x (small samples), and 12x with a 7x to 20x range if you are not sure. The 16.1x figure is used only for targets above 50,000 wishlists.

    Does recent follower growth predict launch sales better than the total?

    Not on its own. In Chris Zukowski's October 2026 analysis of 160 games, total launch wishlists reached an R-squared of 0.73, wishlist adds in the last 30 days 0.72, and the two combined 0.75. Those figures measure wishlist adds, not follower adds, and follower velocity has not been tested in any cited source. Recent pace still shows momentum, so the calculator uses your last 30 days as today's pace and lets it decay.

    Is a wishlist worth as much as it used to be?

    No. In Chris Zukowski's October 2026 data on 160 reader-submitted games, wishlists for 2023 to 2025 releases convert to sales at about half the rate of 2020 to 2022 releases. Festival and tooltip wishlisting explain part of the drop. Treat it as directional, since readers submitted the sample.

    Notes
    • Experimental tool. This calculator is based on publicly available industry data, but the underlying metrics are subject to significant variance, genre-specific differences, and market conditions that no dataset fully captures. Use it to explore possible scenarios and pressure-test assumptions. Don't use it as a basis for financial decisions without cross-referencing with your own data and professional advice.
    • Wishlist estimates are approximations. The follower to wishlist ratio varies by genre, festival participation, and how long the page has been open. It has also risen over time, because marketers now push wishlist calls-to-action harder than follows. GameDiscoverCo surveys of unreleased games put the median at 9.6x in 2021 (range 5x to 14x) and at 12x in 2023 (range 7x to 20x, 125+ games, unreleased titles of all sizes). For games released in the previous 12 months with more than 50,000 launch wishlists (biggest outliers removed), the May 2026 median reached 16.1x. In a 600+ game sample of that size class, the 100 highest-multiplier games converted their launch wishlist balance to Month 1 sales at a median 0.22x, against 0.37x for the 100 lowest (68% better). GameDiscoverCo says too many variables are involved to read that as cause and effect, and expects casual or crew-style games to sit above the median. The 12x all-game median and the 7x to 20x range on this page come from the 2023 data (unreleased games of all sizes). The two game-type buckets average only six tags from that survey, so treat them as a rough lean, not a prediction. Games with recent Next Fest or demo exposure tend to sit higher. Sources: GameDiscoverCo, 2021 · GameDiscoverCo, Jun 2023 · Impress Games, 2023 · GameDiscoverCo, May 2026
    • Recent pace shows momentum. A page that gained 200 followers last month shows more momentum than one that collected 10,000 over three years and has since gone quiet. GameDiscoverCo compared a hand-picked set of under-converting titles with a hand-picked top 20 and found the under-converters had spent far longer in pre-release, an average of 411 days against 214 (median 7-day review score 67% against 91%). That is a comparison of two chosen groups, not a general rule. On recent pace, Chris Zukowski's October 2026 analysis of 160 games found, pooled across eras and on a log-log basis, that total wishlists at launch reached an R² of 0.73, wishlist adds in the last 30 days 0.72, 7-day adds 0.68, and total plus 30-day adds 0.75 (primary post). Those figures measure wishlist adds, not follower adds. Follower velocity has not been tested in any cited source. The calculator uses your last 30 days as a growth rate when you enter them. Sources: GameDiscoverCo, Oct 2025 (paywalled), GameDiscoverCo, Apr 2024 and Game World Observer, 15 Oct 2026.
    • Inflation changed what a wishlist is worth. Wishlists for recent releases convert to sales at about half the rate they did for 2020-22 releases, according to Chris Zukowski's Oct 2026 data on 160 reader-submitted games (34 from 2020 to 2022, 126 from 2023 to 2025), so treat the figure as directional. Festival and tooltip wishlisting drive part of that drop. The sources disagree: GameDiscoverCo (Oct 2025, partly paywalled) reports no evidence that conversion is degrading over time, while Zukowski reports it halved. They use different samples and definitions, and this page does not resolve the conflict. Sources: Game World Observer analysis and Chris Zukowski, How to Market a Game.
    • How the projection works. Followers are projected with a pace that decays over time plus separately labelled bursts (Next Fest, demo, trailer, press), each capped. No source gives follower decay constants, burst sizes or the spread of follower pace, so those values are this tool's own adjustable assumptions, shown under "Model assumptions" and in "Formulas and constants". The low, high and gap figures stack assumptions in one direction. They are scenarios, not a tested error band, and this page has not been validated against real launches.
    • Spikes aren't guaranteed. Next Fest, demos, and trailers can produce large follower boosts, but results vary enormously by genre, timing, and execution. Use spike estimates conservatively.
    • Estimation only. Directional indicator, not a forecast. Real planning needs your actual Steamworks data.
